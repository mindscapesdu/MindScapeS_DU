Colloquium Profile

About the Series

The MindScapeS Frontier Talk Series on Cognition is an international academic forum hosted by the Department of Philosophy at the University of Delhi.

Mission & Academic Scope

The series was established to foster rigorous, sustained inquiry into the foundational nature of human and non-human cognition. By placing cutting-edge empirical findings from developmental psychology, cognitive neuroscience, and artificial intelligence into conversation with conceptual frameworks from the philosophy of mind, epistemology, and philosophical logic, MindScapeS serves as an intellectual nexus for cross-disciplinary dialogue.

Key thematic investigations explored throughout the colloquia include:

  • Mental Architecture & Representation: The nature of object files, concepts, the Language of Thought hypothesis, and non-conceptual content.
  • Perspectives & Perceptual Epistemology: Perspectival flexibility, cognitive schemas, and the epistemic role of perceptual experience.
  • Developmental & Evolutionary Cognition: The ontogeny of core knowledge systems, numerical tracking, and early childhood conceptual development.
  • The Mind-World Interface: How embodied agents select, filter, and structure information from dynamic sensory environments.

Institutional Host

Department of Philosophy, University of Delhi

The Department of Philosophy at the University of Delhi is one of India's premier centers for philosophical education and advanced research. Housing active communities working across both Indian and Western traditions—including analytic philosophy of language, philosophy of mind, phenomenology, epistemology, and ethics—the Department regularly hosts leading international thinkers and colloquia.

Location: Faculty of Arts, University of Delhi, Delhi 110007, India.

Session Format & Modality

Each MindScapeS session is organized as an in-depth seminar designed to balance authoritative exposition with collaborative critique:

Part I (45–50 min)

Keynote Presentation

Comprehensive exposition of new or unpublished research by the featured scholar.

Part II (10–15 min)

Invited Commentary

Formal critical commentary from an invited peer specialist in the field.

Part III (30–40 min)

Open Floor Q&A

Interactive, moderated discussion with participating students and international attendees.

Colloquia are conducted virtually via Zoom, and participation is open to scholars, graduate researchers, undergraduate students, and members of the public free of charge.
