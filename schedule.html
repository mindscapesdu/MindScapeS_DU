Concluded • Session 1

Working Memory Representations and Object File Tracking in Infancy

Prof. Melissa Kibbe • Boston University
Commentator: Prof. Sami Yousif (The Ohio State University)

Abstract

An inaugural colloquium examining the cognitive architecture of object representation, feature binding, and conceptual development in infants and young children.

The discussion explores how working memory tracks discrete object files and how computational constraints inform models of mental representation.

Active • Session 2

Perceptual Capacities and the Grounds of Mental Evidence

Prof. Susanna Schellenberg • Rutgers University
Commentator: Prof. Tarun

Abstract

This paper develops an account of perspectives that explains both perspectival stability and flexibility informed by research in neuroscience, cognitive psychology, and philosophy of mind. Any individual has limited processing resources, and so selects and represents only information pertinent to the situation or task at hand, filtering out information that can safely be ignored.

An individual’s active perspective is anchored, for example, by her self-concept currently in force. This perspectival anchor can shift from moment to moment as she navigates her environment, thus yielding a change in her active perspective. However, the extent to which an individual’s active perspective can shift is restricted by her overall cognitive, perceptual, emotional and agential schemas, which constitute what we call her dormant perspective. Thus, while an individual’s dormant perspective changes only slowly over time, her active perspective can change abruptly.
